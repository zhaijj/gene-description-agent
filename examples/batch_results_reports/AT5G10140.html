Function Summary:
The Arabidopsis thaliana gene AT5G10140, most prominently known as FLOWERING LOCUS C (FLC), and also referred to as AGL25 or FLF, is a master MADS-box transcription factor that acts as a central repressor of flowering. Its primary molecular function is to delay the floral transition by directly binding to and inhibiting the transcription of key flowering-promoter genes, including *FLOWERING LOCUS T* (FT) and *SUPPRESSOR OF OVEREXPRESSION OF CONSTANS 1* (SOC1). The expression of FLC itself is exquisitely regulated by environmental cues, most notably by vernalization, where prolonged exposure to cold leads to its stable epigenetic silencing through histone modifications, thus permitting flowering in the spring. Recent studies have revealed further layers of its regulation, including control by WRKY1, m6A RNA modification, and its role in integrating nitrogen availability signals into the flowering time pathway. Consequently, mutations in FLC result in an early flowering phenotype, while its overexpression causes delayed flowering, highlighting its critical role in aligning plant reproduction with favorable seasonal conditions.
